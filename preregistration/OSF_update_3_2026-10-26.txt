Update 3 to the OSF registration of the Pass-3 assessment protocol (version 1.2)
================================================================================

Registration: Assessment Protocol For A Survey Of Published 1H NMR Descriptions (Pass 3,
Version 1.2), OSF Registries, registered 15 October 2026, doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/EQ9AH

This update records what has been decided or added since Update 2. It does not change the
checklist items 1-7, the sample or the primary analysis. Files referred to below are in the
public GitHub repository ArturEshtukov-Sheglov/nmr-1h-description-tools, folder

1. Status at the time of writing
--------------------------------
- Reference: the author has reviewed 307 of the 982 first-pass decisions; the review of the
  remaining 675 is in progress.
- Blind test, external rater, comparison models (GPT, Qwen, DeepSeek): unchanged since
  Update 2.
- Second data set (Update 1, section 7): not yet registered; no article of it has been opened.


2. Version 2.1 of the tools
---------------------------
(a) The results of the paper remain those of the frozen version (Update 1, section 2(a)).
    Version 2.1 (V2.1-beta, frozen 26 October 2026) is not used for any result of the Organic
    Letters set. It is called a beta until its accuracy has been measured on the test articles of
    the second data set (section 3(e)); the public web version (Update 2, section 5(g)) will run it.
(b) V2.1 changes results. Changes in reading the descriptions: notations not read by V2 (for
    example "mc", "stack", "complex", "c.s.", "quasi d", "hextet", "nfod" with J_app, "br-s",
    signals without brackets, the multiplicity written before the bracket), several shifts or
    ranges given with one bracket ("2.09, 2.08, 2.01 (3 x s, each 3H)"), coupling constants
    each followed by an assignment, the end of a description (data of other nuclei written
    without "NMR", MS, UV-vis and IR data, procedure text after the last signal), running page
    headers of journals inside a description, and assignments beginning with a digit read as
    coupling constants. Changes in the checks: item 1 - protons on boron (B-H) not reported are
    recorded in the protocol subcategory "protons on quadrupolar nuclei not reported"; when no
    structure is available and the solvent is protic (D2O, CD3OD), fewer protons than the
    formula are recorded in the subcategory "exchangeable proton not reported"; descriptions
    that list only some signals ("characteristic peaks") are "cannot be determined";
    descriptions of mixtures are recognised by more words; a structure obtained from a class
    word taken as the compound name (e.g. "Carbamate") is not used. Item 4(a) - "pt" and "pq"
    are read as pseudo-triplet and pseudo-quartet when the constants show this. Item 6(b) -
    shifts that the authors give together with one bracket are not treated as two first-order
    signals. V2.1 also keeps repeated identical signals (section 5(a)).
(c) The full list of changes, with the date, the data source in which each failure was found
    and the test added for it, is in DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md (EN) / DEVELOPMENT_LOG_RU.md (RU) and
    CHANGELOG.md of the code.


3. Development data of V2.1
---------------------------
(a) V2.1 was developed on open-access articles of 2024-2025 from the PubMed Central Open Access
    Subset, CC BY licence only, downloaded with the script fetch_oa_si.py (PMC E-utilities and
    the PMC Cloud Service): Beilstein J. Org. Chem. (109 articles, supporting information),
    ACS Omega (170), J. Org. Chem. (300; supporting information and article PDFs) and Molecules
    (1,004 article PDFs; 500 at random and 504 selected with the full-text search term
    "CDCl3 OR DMSO-d6"). The lists of PMCIDs are deposited:
    DEVELOPMENT_DATA_BJOC_PMCIDs.txt, DEVELOPMENT_DATA_ACSOMEGA_PMCIDs.txt,
    DEVELOPMENT_DATA_JOC_PMCIDs.txt, DEVELOPMENT_DATA_MOLECULES_PMCIDs.txt.
    In total these files contain 15,723 1H descriptions found by V2.1.
(b) As for V2, the 201 SI files of the Organic Letters sample were used for regression checks
    and the 982 reference descriptions with their structures for comparison; V2.1 figures on
    these data are reported for information only and are not measures of accuracy.
(c) Journals that are or may be part of this survey are never used as development data:
    Org. Lett., Angew. Chem. Int. Ed., Eur. J. Org. Chem., Tetrahedron Lett., Chem. Commun.,
    Nature Communications and Communications Chemistry (any year). The download script refuses
    them. J. Org. Chem. is not part of the survey.
(d) No article published in 2026 was used for development.
(e) The accuracy of V2.1 will be measured only on the test articles of the second data set,
    under the update that registers that set (Update 1, section 7), as for V2.

5. Known limitations of the frozen version found during development of V2.1
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) Repeated identical signals. The frozen version (and versions 1.1, V2 and V2.0.1) reads a
    signal that appears twice in a description with exactly the same text (e.g. "3.80 (s, 3H,
    OMe PMB), 3.80 (s, 3H, OMe PMB)") only once: the repeat was treated as a copy of the text.
    In published descriptions such repeats are different groups with the same signal (the OMe
    groups of two PMB groups, three TBS groups, keto and enol forms of one compound). Dropping
    the repeat can give a false item-1 finding (too few protons) and can change the pairs
    examined under items 3 and 6(b).
    How and when it was found: on 23 October 2026, during development of version 2.1, in a
    stress test with synthetic descriptions written from known structures
    (tests/stress/structure_test.py): all false item-1 findings on correct synthetic
    descriptions came from this behaviour. At that time the first pass of the 982 descriptions
    was complete and the author's review had covered 307 of them. The author's reference
    assessment is not affected (the author reads every signal).
    Extent: 83 of the 8,609 descriptions found in the 201 SI files, and 15 of the 982 selected
    descriptions, contain a repeated identical signal.
    Handling: the frozen version is not changed, and the tool results of the paper remain those
    of the frozen version. In the accuracy analysis of the frozen version against the
    reference, the number of disagreements under items 1, 3 and 6(b) caused by this behaviour
    among the 15 descriptions will be reported. From version 2.1, repeated signals are kept.
(b) HRMS data written before the 1H description. In two of the 201 SI files (SI035, SI181) the
    HRMS of each compound is written before its 1H description. The frozen version takes the
    HRMS formula that follows a description, so in these files it attaches each formula to the
    neighbouring compound. None of the 982 selected descriptions is affected. V2.1 recognises
    this layout per file, from the agreement of the integrals with the formula.


6. Stress tests
---------------
(a) The self-test of the code (python run_all.py --selftest) runs unit tests and four stress
    tests with synthetic descriptions and a fixed seed: a format test, a test of the checks
    with known errors in spin systems, a test of the structure checks with descriptions
    written from known structures, and, from V2.1, a realistic reading test.
(b) In the realistic reading test the content of one published description (shifts,
    multiplicities, coupling constants, integrals) is written in the style of another
    published description (header, separators, order of the parts in the bracket, spellings of
    multiplicities, templates of the coupling constants, assignments), with the notation
    variants and their frequencies taken from the 15,723 development descriptions (the 12,830
    descriptions that V2.1 reads completely give the styles and contents). The correct answer
    is known for every synthetic description. With 100,000 descriptions and seed 260626,
    V2.1-beta misreads 39 (0.04 %) and V2 7,017 (7.02 %). The self-test of V2.1-beta with
    10,000 descriptions per test (seed 20261023) passes all checks.
(c) Stress tests are not accuracy measurements. They show how robustly the tool reads the
    forms that occur in the literature, with the correct answer known for every description.
    They do not show how accurate the tool is on new articles: the accuracy of each version is
    measured only on test articles that were not used in its development.
