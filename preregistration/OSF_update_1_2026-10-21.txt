Update 1 to the OSF registration of the Pass-3 assessment protocol (version 1.2)
================================================================================

Registration: Assessment Protocol For A Survey Of Published 1H NMR Descriptions (Pass 3,
Version 1.2), OSF Registries, registered 15 October 2026, doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/EQ9AH
Author: Artur V. Eshtukov-Shcheglov
Date written: 21 October 2026

This update records, in one place, everything that has been added to or decided about the
registered plan since 15 October 2026, and what had been seen at each point. It does not
change the checklist items 1-7, the sample or the primary analysis. Files referred to below
are in the public GitHub repository ArturEshtukov-Sheglov/nmr-1h-description-tools, folder
preregistration/, with the commits and SHA-256 values given here.


1. Status at the time of writing
--------------------------------
- Sample: unchanged (Organic Letters, first half of 2026; 200 articles; 982 descriptions
  selected at random, seed 260626).
- First pass: an assessment of all 982 descriptions under protocol v1.2 was made with the
  assistance of a language model (Claude, Anthropic) on 16 October 2026, after the
  registration. Its summary figures were computed and seen by the author.
- Reference: the author's review of every first-pass decision started on 18 October 2026 and
  is in progress. The reference is the reviewed table, not the first pass.
- Module S (description vs published spectrum, one compound per article): run on
  16 October 2026; the rule clarifications listed in section 4 were made during that run.
- Comparison models (GPT, Qwen, DeepSeek): not yet run.
- Blind test (20 descriptions drawn at random from the selected descriptions, seed
  "260626:blind:OrgLett"): the author assessed all 20 on paper on 15 October 2026, without
  seeing any first-pass decision (the first pass was made on 16 October); the sheets are
  signed and dated. The answers have not yet been transcribed or compared with the first
  pass. The external rater's assessment is not yet completed. A scan of the author's sheets
  will be deposited together with the external rater's answers, before either is compared
  with the first pass or the reference.

3. Secondary analysis of the full set (added 18 October 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------
The plan in preregistration/OSF_amendment_fullset_2026-10-18.txt (commit 1e31eba) is part of
this registration. It was written and published before any summary of the tool's checks on
the full set was computed. In brief: the tool's flags on all 8,609 descriptions found in the
200 SI files are reported overall and per item (1, 3, 4(a), 6), with an estimate corrected for
the tool's sensitivity and specificity measured on the 982 reference descriptions; flag rates
of descriptions marked as possibly known and of unmarked descriptions are compared; items 2,
4(b) and 7 are not estimated for the full set; the primary analysis is unchanged.

Added after the raw full-set flags had been computed (descriptive, not a test of any
hypothesis): the distribution over articles of the share of each article's descriptions
that are flagged (median, interquartile range, number of articles with no flag and with
50 % or more), because pooled shares give large articles more weight.


4. Rule clarifications and application decisions (protocol v1.2 unchanged)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each was made before the author's review of the corresponding decisions. Where it was made
after results had been seen, this is stated.

(a) 15 October 2026 (trial on two articles outside the sample, before any sample description
    was assessed): an item-3 mismatch inside a system that is already non-first-order under
    item 6 remains an item-3 inconsistency, and the number of item-3 findings that lie only in
    such signals is reported; a constant without a partner counts as published unless the
    authors give an explanation with the description.
(b) Module S, after the 25-compound pilot and before the full module-S run:
    - S3: a reported shift stands for its rounding interval; it is consistent if that
      interval, widened by one unit of the last decimal of the spectrum label, overlaps the
      labelled lines;
    - S1: signals inconsistent or borderline under item 6 are excluded; isolated AB pairs are
      checked;
    - S2: a solvent or water peak inside an integral region makes that signal "cannot be
      determined"; a difference of 0.5 or more is an inconsistency unless the description
      explains it;
    - module S is applied to one selected compound per article, drawn with
      random.Random("260626:moduleS:" + DOI).
(c) Module S, after the full module-S run had been seen (16-17 October 2026):
    - S3: truncation is treated like rounding (6.797 reported as 6.79 is consistent);
    - S4: an evident typo in a spectrum caption (e.g. "400 Hz", "CD3Cl") is not an
      inconsistency when the spectrum shows the same frequency or solvent.
    Both changes reduce the number of module-S findings. Each changed the verdict of one
    compound: 31 of 199 assessable compounds are inconsistent with the changes and 33 of 199
    without them. Module-S results are reported both ways.
(d) 18 October 2026, item 6: the terminal CH3 of a saturated unbranched alkyl chain of five
    or more carbons reported in first-order notation is recorded in the subcategory
    "formally non-first-order, flexible chain" (not an inconsistency). Made before the
    author's review; it changed one first-pass verdict, in a subcategory only.
(e) Open questions to be decided before the reference is finalised, and recorded in a
    further update: (1) item 7 for first-order-looking triplets of aromatic protons whose
    partners lie in the borderline strong-coupling zone of item 6(b); (2) item 4(c) for
    couplings that cannot remain unresolved (e.g. 2J(H,F) of about 47 Hz in CH2F groups).
    Results will be reported under the decision taken, and the number of descriptions
    affected under the alternative will be given.


5. Statistics (clarification)
-----------------------------
Descriptions from the same article are not independent. All confidence intervals for
description-level shares are obtained by an article-level (cluster) percentile bootstrap:
articles are resampled with replacement (10,000 resamples, seed 260626) and all selected
descriptions of each resampled article are kept. The design effect and intraclass
correlation are reported. Article-level measures (share of articles with at least one
inconsistent description; per-article shares) are bootstrapped over articles in the same way.
Shares weighted by n/k (number of descriptions in the article / number selected) are reported
alongside unweighted shares. Module S has one compound per article, so its article-level
bootstrap equals an ordinary bootstrap; the Wilson interval is given as a check.
